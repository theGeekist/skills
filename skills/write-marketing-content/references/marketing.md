# Communicate customer value accurately

Start with the audience's situation and the decision or action the content should support. Infer these from the brief and available sources before asking for missing information. Preserve the product's actual domain meaning while using words the audience recognises.

## Build the claim from the sources

Separate capabilities available now, announced plans, experiments and illustrative scenarios. Link internal drafting notes to supporting sources where useful. Keep operational caveats visible when they affect the buyer's decision, without making implementation diagnostics the headline of customer copy.

Explain value in concrete terms: who can do what, under which conditions, and why it matters. Avoid claiming a measured improvement, customer result, assurance or integration that the supplied evidence does not establish. Unsupported superlatives cannot substitute for a useful explanation.

Select limitations by their effect on the audience's choice. Do not reproduce an inventory of unpromised absent features just because the source lists them. Preserve a material security, eligibility or trial constraint near the relevant action, and keep detailed product scope in an appropriate linked route when available.

## Choose the form

| Form | Reader route |
| --- | --- |
| Landing or product page copy | Reader's need → value → credible support → action |
| Announcement | What is available → who it helps → relevant behaviour → next step |
| Positioning brief | Audience and alternatives → problem → differentiated value → supporting capabilities |
| Technical-to-commercial explanation | Purpose → practical effect → important boundary → deeper technical source |

Keep tone appropriate to the audience and channel. A sales claim, editorial essay and short product blurb need different cadence. Preserve the requested voice where it is compatible with accurate claims.

## Translate internal states

Use the project's audience labels when available. An internal qualification state may need an explanation of progress and next steps rather than a raw database label. Do not collapse distinct concepts such as confidence, coverage and eligibility into one percentage.

When evidence for a requested claim is missing, draft supported value and record the specific validation or source needed for the stronger claim. Continue useful drafting rather than disqualifying the whole campaign. Keep claim notes separate from finished customer copy unless the user requested an annotated draft.
