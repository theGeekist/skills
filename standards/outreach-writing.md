# Outreach writing — Sources, voice and editorial examples

## What belongs to the skill

A message should make a meaningful observation or request in an individual's own voice, grounded in their actual relationship and evidence. The skill uses real correspondence when available, especially the author's own edits. It owns the message and editorial judgement, not candidate discovery, CRM record schemas, scheduling or delivery.

For one-off revisions, use the supplied draft and corrections. For batches, consult representative real correspondence across relationship types. A connected CRM can provide history; the skill does not require a particular CRM, storage provider or author identity.

## Preserve editorial learning across tasks

When an author makes a consequential correction, identify the reasoning that changed: emphasis, logical progression, intent, attribution or register. If a persistent voice reference is authorised and available, store the transferable lesson with the original passage and context so future workers can apply the judgement without imitating the wording. The invoking workflow owns the location and permission to update that reference.

Never translate an author's contextual edits into banned-phrase lists, synonym swaps or universal templates. Do not write to a profile or CRM solely because a draft was revised.

## Contextual editing examples

**"I particularly like" → "I like"**

The following details already explain why the work is appreciated. Removing emphasis respects the observation rather than weakening it.

**"But then I stumbled upon" → "Then I stumbled upon"**

The new discovery continues the investigation rather than contradicting the preceding idea. Removing "But" restores the actual sequence.

These are explanations of editorial judgement, not bans on the words "particularly" or "but". Likewise, the phrase "I was looking at your website" can be filler or an authentic description of what happened.

Learn principles from the author's decisions and corrections, not superficial string replacements or a fixed tone model.
